I am an undergraduate in Robotics Engineering at [Peking University](https://english.pku.edu.cn/), expecting my B.E. in 2027; I started there in Economics before moving to robotics. Since June 2026 I have been a student intern at the [Robotics Institute](https://www.ri.cmu.edu/), Carnegie Mellon University.

I work in applied mathematics. My interest is in problems where a system's behaviour is governed by structure that can be pinned down exactly — and where the right geometric or analytic argument replaces a numerical search with a theorem. Most of my work so far has taken this view to robotics and control: classifying the singularity varieties of redundant manipulators by projective line geometry, characterizing what a Lyapunov–Razumikhin condition can and cannot certify about a delayed system, and quantifying the metric distortion of a randomly initialized network layer. I also work on collective robotics, where the same questions appear in a swarm rather than in a single mechanism.

**Research directions.**

- **Geometry and algebra of mechanisms.** Singularity varieties of redundant serial chains, screw and line geometry, and geometric conditions for hoverability and robustness of aerial vehicles.
- **Analysis of dynamical and delay systems.** Lyapunov–Razumikhin and input-to-state stability theory, and what a given class of stability certificate can actually decide.
- **Quantitative analysis of high-dimensional maps.** Bi-Lipschitz constants, conditioning, and metric distortion in randomly initialized networks.

Beyond these, I read broadly in operations research, game theory, information theory, statistical physics, and fluid and computational mechanics; a fuller list is on my [CV](/cv/).

I am currently looking for research collaborations, and I am applying for PhD positions starting in Fall 2027. I am glad to hear from anyone working on related problems — the best way to reach me is by [email](mailto:liyaoc@andrew.cmu.edu).
